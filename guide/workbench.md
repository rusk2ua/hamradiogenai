# The workbench (October 2026)

Like choosing a transceiver for a band, pick the tool for the job. This is my experience on my own projects; test yours against your own source material.

| Tool | What I use it for | My verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Claude** (desktop app, web, mobile) | Multi-file projects in local folders, code, tests, documentation, slide decks, websites | **Primary.** Covers nearly everything I do |
| **Gemini Notebook** | Source-grounded research, image generation, podcast-style audio overviews | **Very much worth a look**, especially for learning a topic and for media |
| **Kiro** | Spec-first IDE (a VS Code fork) for people who live in an editor | Rarely, now. Claude covers what I used it for |
| **ChatGPT** | Complex technical work | **Hard no.** Consistently poor results for me, most recently a schematic that stayed wrong through multiple rounds of correction |

## What changed since the paper (August 2026)

- **"Claude Cowork" is simply part of Claude now.** Point the desktop app at a project folder and it reads, writes and runs code there, and keeps working while you step away.
- **House rules, written once.** Skills and project instructions carry your conventions (versioning, README style, commit identity) into every session.
- **Memory across chats.** It remembers your station, your repos and your decisions, so you re-explain less.
